MEET ALINE

Before becoming a trauma-informed somatic counsellor, I spent years working in digital analytics. My work centred around understanding behaviour, identifying patterns and asking thoughtful questions.

Today, I combine that analytical mindset with trauma-informed somatic counselling, helping people better understand themselves through both conversation and the body.

Like many people I work with, I've experienced periods where life felt overwhelming.

I've navigated anxiety, experienced profound grief and learned that understanding something intellectually doesn't always change how it feels in the body.

Through those experiences I discovered the importance of nervous system regulation and movement — not as quick fixes, but as ways of gradually rebuilding trust in myself.

Those experiences continue to shape how I work today.

I offer counselling in both English and Dutch, online and in person.

WHAT SETS MY WORK APART

SCIENCE WITH HUMANITY

Trauma-informed somatic counselling is grounded in growing research on the nervous system and the connection between our body, emotions and experiences. I believe understanding the science helps us make sense of our responses. Not to reduce people to theories, but to approach each person's story with greater compassion, curiosity and care.

MOVEMENT AS MEDICINE

Movement has always been part of my own life, not as a way to perform or achieve more, but as a way to reconnect with myself. Whether that's yoga, walking, running or simply learning to notice your body, movement can become a powerful resource for regulation, resilience and wellbeing. When appropriate, I integrate movement into our work in a way that feels supportive and accessible to you.

UNDERSTANDING PATTERNS

Before becoming a counsellor, I spent six years working in digital analytics, helping organisations better understand human behaviour through data. That curiosity has stayed with me. Today, rather than searching for patterns in numbers, I help people make sense of the patterns within themselves. Bringing together empathy, structure and thoughtful reflection rather than assumptions or judgement.
