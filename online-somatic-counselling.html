ONLINE SESSIONS

Your environment
can become part of the work.

Online sessions offer the same 75-minute space for conversation, body awareness and exploration as in-person sessions.

We work with what is happening in your life while also paying attention to how it shows up in your body.

You don't need any previous experience with somatic work, yoga or meditation.

You simply need a private space where you feel comfortable being yourself.

HOW IT WORKS

A session can be
simple.

Most of the session involves conversation. We start with what's happening in your life, what brought you here and what you'd like to understand or change.

At certain moments, we may slow down and notice what's happening physically as you talk. Perhaps your breathing changes. Your shoulders tense. You notice something in your stomach. Or you suddenly feel the urge to move, withdraw or become still. Depending on what feels useful, we might explore grounding, breath awareness, posture or gentle movement.

Nothing is forced. The work follows what you need in that moment.

WHY ONLINE?

There can be something
valuable about working
from home.

You're not stepping into a completely separate world for 75 minutes.

You're already in a space you know, surrounded by the things that are part of your everyday life.

That can make online counselling feel natural and accessible, particularly if you feel more comfortable beginning this work from home.

It can also mean that after a session, you don't immediately have to step back into the outside world.

You can take a little time to pause, integrate and transition back into your day.

THE SCIENCE

Online doesn't mean
less personal.

Research on online psychotherapy has found that therapeutic outcomes and the therapeutic relationship can be comparable to in-person work.

For many people, the familiarity and privacy of their own space can make online sessions a natural way to begin.

FIND WHAT WORKS FOR YOU

Online or
in Amsterdam?

Some people prefer the familiarity of home.

Others prefer stepping outside their usual environment and meeting in person.

Neither is inherently better.

What matters is finding the setting in which you feel most comfortable exploring what you're experiencing.
